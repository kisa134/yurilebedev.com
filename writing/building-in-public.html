Essay 002 · Trading & building

Why I’m building my trading tools in public

Trading is the work. The terminal is the product. Writing is how I want to make my thinking visible.

CogitoOctober 2026

I trade every day. I also have my own trading terminal, a product that brings together my trading tools and the ideas I want to develop further.

Alongside that work, I study systems thinking, apply the First Principles Framework, and use AI to help me work through questions. There is a lot happening in private. I want to start making the useful parts public.

That is the reason for this site. Not to become a full-time content creator. Not to turn every trading session into a performance. To write clearly about work I am already doing.

The work comes first

Trading remains my main focus. The terminal is my main product. This journal should support both, rather than become a separate machine that consumes the time they need.

I want to share selected questions from that work: what I’m trying to solve, why a tool needs to exist, what an experiment can actually tell me, and what remains unresolved.

Some entries will be about a specific tool. Others will be about a decision, an idea I’m studying, or a change in how I approach a problem. I also build games with collaborators, so there will be occasional notes from those projects.

A terminal, not a promise

The terminal gives this journal a concrete centre. Instead of only discussing ideas, I can connect them to a product that is being developed.

But showing a product is not the same as demonstrating an advantage in the market. I don’t want polished screenshots or confident language to stand in for evidence.

When I share an experiment, my aim is to make its status clear: an idea, something implemented, a test, or a result with limits. Where there is not enough evidence, that should remain visible.

Thinking that leaves a trace

For me, the point of studying a framework is to change how I work. I want to write about that connection in ordinary language, through specific questions and examples, rather than ask every reader to learn a vocabulary first.

A useful note might explain which assumption I questioned, which alternatives I considered, or what information would make me change my mind. The intention is to make the reasoning easier to inspect, including the parts that are incomplete.

I want the writing to leave a clearer record of the work.

A simple publishing practice

I’m starting with a deliberately simple format: a daily article or focused field note here, and a short post on X that carries its main idea and points to the full piece.

I can add other formats later. For now, I want a place to write, an archive that grows, and conversations with people who care about trading, tools, and thoughtful work.

The next entries should make the work more concrete.

This is a personal journal about process and product development. It is not a signal service, an offer to manage money, or a promise of trading performance.
